## 1. Core Strategic Pivot: How We Frame the Paper to Win Over `birj`, `HE3D`, and `ihsT`

All three reviewers respected the paper's empirical honesty on negative results, but pushed back hard on **three tensions between the claims and the data**:
1. **The 256K Headline Tension (`birj` W1, `ihsT` W3/W4):** The abstract highlights the primary Polar checkpoint's `34.4%` token / `9.0%` exact retrieval at 256K, even though (a) all `9.0%` is on synthetic filler (`0.0%` on natural text from 32K onward), (b) the two fresh 9.8B replications reach `16.97%`/`20.90%` token and `0.00%`/`3.83%` exact at 256K, and (c) once NoPE's single saturating memory head is capped (`hl:256`), **capped NoPE (`1.506 / 1.490` BPB) beats Polar (`1.644 / 1.762` BPB) at 256K** while also having better 2K short-context quality (`45.15%` vs `43.29%`).
2. **The Component Attribution Tension (`HE3D` W1, `ihsT` W2/Q2):** Sections 5.2 and 6.4 claimed E1 established the necessity of both direction normalization and the null competitor, even though 3-arm E1 only compared full Polar to Temp-Softmax, and Table 7 (a single-seed 16-trial pilot) showed fixing the null slope near zero improved 64K retrieval (`91.25%` vs `77.50%`).
3. **The Numerical Consistency Tension (`HE3D` W2/Q1, `ihsT` W6/Q2):** Unexplained discrepancies between Table 20 (`1.619 s`) and Table 33 (`0.065 s`), between Table 2 (`1.825 -> 1.501`) and E2 (`1.855 -> 1.520`), and between Figure 4 (`10.77`) and Table 23 (`12.003`).

### The Winning Reframing (Revised Abstract)

> **Proposed Revised Abstract:**
> What determines whether a hybrid language model extrapolates beyond its training length? Using **ATMA**—a 378M-parameter hybrid combining local gated convolutions, global attention (NoPE, RoPE, or **Polar Attention**), and gated-delta recurrent memory—we show that long-context sequence likelihood and associative retrieval are governed by two distinct mechanisms. In a complete 120-cell, 1B-token factorial trained at 2K, adding recurrent memory improves Polar's 64K retrieval in all 20 matched cells (+47.8 percentage points on average), whereas RoPE remains at 0.0%. Across three matched 9.816B-token NoPE/Polar training pairs evaluated on 30 independent natural documents, Polar consistently delays the loss of exact natural-text retrieval across 8K–64K, outperforming NoPE by **+37.27 percentage points** `[95% cluster bootstrap CI: +32.08%, +42.55%]` (**30/30** documents; `+35.42%`, `+36.81%`, and `+39.58%` per pair). Extending pretraining $4\times$ to 8K (`seq_len = 8192`) scales Polar's 8K–64K natural-text exact retrieval to **54.86%** (**51.11%** at 64K vs. **21.11%** for learned-temperature/Scalable-Softmax, **1.11%** for NoPE, and **0.00%** for RoPE). By contrast, at extreme lengths (256K) from 2K pretraining, exact natural-text retrieval falls to 0.0% for all unadapted models, and synthetic exact retrieval varies substantially across runs (9.00% primary vs. 0.00% and 3.83% in fresh seed-paired replications; 16.97%–34.37% token accuracy).
>
> Conversely, we find that **256K likelihood degradation is primarily a recurrent retention-boundary failure rather than an attention failure**. Checkpoints with nearly identical 2K validation loss diverge sharply in 256K bits per byte (BPB) when isolated gated-delta heads learn near-unit retention gates ($\gamma_0 \to 1$, implying $10^5$–$10^7$ token half-lives). An inference-only 256-token half-life cap on the single most retentive head repairs NoPE's 256K BPB across all three runs (`8.097 -> 1.595` primary; `3.898/5.256 -> 1.506/1.490` replications, matching or outperforming Polar's `1.521–1.855` BPB), while alternative-head controls yield 0.0 recovery. Crucially, however, repairing NoPE's retention horizon does not rescue its natural-text retrieval (0.0% from 16K onward). A matched 18-run (6-arm $\times$ 3-seed) component ablation isolates the contributions of Polar's magnitude channel (`+23.84` points over direction-only), learned $\sqrt{\log(n+1)}$ null-sink scaling (`+5.28` points overall and `+10.56` points at 64K over fixed null slope), and full Polar readout over Scalable-Softmax (`+22.18` points), alongside Polar's 1.86-point short-context trade-off and KV-cache decode cost.
